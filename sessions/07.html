Retention math

Cheaper — in a range, not a commandment

Writers often say acquiring a new customer costs anywhere from about 5 to 25 times more than keeping one. The band depends on the industry and the study. Compute yours.

What to compute

  • CAC this quarter
  • Cost to run loyalty + support (CRC)
  • Repeat contribution from existing guests

What not to do

  • Quote “5×” as if it were PSAK
  • Keep a toxic customer because retention is ‘cheaper’
  • Ignore the free-drink COGS

Amy Gallo, Harvard Business Review, 29 Oct 2014, summarising acquisition-vs-retention studies as “five to 25 times” depending on study and industry. Directional, not a constant.

This fixes the S07 inconsistency. Reichheld 5% retention / 25–95% profit is optional colour if they ask — also HBR 2014 — but it is also a range, not a promise.

Levers

Five retention moves that are not magic

Loyalty

Points or tiers. Starbucks-style. Needs a liability policy if material.

Personalise

History, birthday, restock. Data must be lawful and not creepy.

Support

Answer before they explode. A FAQ is cheaper than a refund war.

Community

WA group, Saturday cupping. Cost is time and the pastry tray.

Feedback

NPS / CSAT, then close the loop. “You asked, we changed the lid.”

Credit hygiene

For B2B: AR aging is a CRM report. Service and collections sit together.

Loyalty on the ledger

A stamp is a tiny contract

If material

Unredeemed points can be a deferred obligation (or a reduction of revenue) under a points programme. Estimate breakage. Review each year.

If a café stamp card

Often immaterial. Still track redemptions so the free cup is COGS, not a mystery inventory shrink. Write the policy in one sentence.
