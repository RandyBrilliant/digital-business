Business Model Canvas

Nine blocks, one story

Partners

Who you cannot live without (roaster, JNE, PSP).

Activities

What you must be good at (brew, reply, reconcile).

Resources

Assets: people, brand, listing, recipes, cash.

Value

The job the customer hires you for.

Relationships

Self-serve, personal, community.

Channels

How they find you and get the cup.

Segments

Who pays. Be cruelly specific.

Cost structure

Fixed vs variable. MDR and ads live here.

Revenue

How money enters — and when PSAK 72 agrees.

Accountants over-index on the last two blocks. Good. Force them to write value and segment first anyway.

Kopi Nusantara canvas · finance cut

Cost and revenue, said plainly

Cost structure

  • Variable: beans, milk, cups, MDR, delivery
  • Semi: part-time barista, ads
  • Fixed: rent, owner draw, software

Revenue streams

  • Cups and pastry at the counter
  • Beans to take home
  • Not: “community” until someone pays
If a block has no number or no test, it is decoration. Strategy is a poster plus a date you will look at the bank.
Students write ‘branding’ as a revenue stream. Kill it.

Lean

Build — measure — learn

Build

Smallest test

Landing page, one SKU, concierge WhatsApp. Weeks, not a custom app.

Measure

Real people

A metric that can prove you wrong. Vanity views do not count.

Learn

Pivot or persist

Write the decision. Then the next loop.

Reid Hoffman’s line — “if you are not embarrassed by v1, you launched too late” — is a prompt, not a licence to ship unsafe food or an unbalanced set of books.

Pivot types in one breath: segment, price, channel, technology. Do not camp here.

Unit economics

The persist / pivot page

Must show

  • Price, COGS, variable fees
  • Contribution per order
  • CAC and a honest payback
  • Fixed cost coverage (how many orders/day)

Red flags

  • GMV as revenue
  • CLV with no repeat data
  • Ads assumed ‘brand’ and excluded
  • Owner salary left off forever
Payback = CAC / contribution per repeat-adjusted month. Keep the algebra simple.

AI in finance · a sober card

Useful automation vs slideware

Useful now

Bank-feed matching, invoice capture, anomaly lists, draft commentaries. A human still signs.

Be careful

Auto-coding GST/PPN, revenue recognition, anything that hits Coretax without review.

Not a strategy

“We will add AI” is not a canvas block. Name the task, the control, the cost.

One slide only. Students will over-claim AI in the capstone otherwise.

Positioning

What you refuse is strategy

Customer

Office 07:30, not ‘everyone who likes coffee’.

Offer

One signature drink and a pastry, not a 40-line menu in month one.

Channel

WhatsApp + Google + one paid test. Not six platforms badly.

A canvas that says yes to every segment is a wish list. Write one explicit ‘we will not’.
Strategy as subtraction. Then capital vs expense.

Capex vs opex, digital edition

Most ‘digital assets’ are still expenses

Usually expense

Ads, retainers, Reels, SaaS seats, domain, most website tweaks. Internally generated brands stay off the balance sheet.

Maybe capitalise

A purchased domain of material value, qualifying software development under the relevant PSAK — only with a paper trail. When unsure, expense and disclose the judgement.

Do not turn this into IAS 38 class. Judgement + documentation. Then studio.

Worked break-even

How many cups cover the week?

Teaching figuresRp
Contribution per ticket (after COGS, MDR, average promo)14,000
Weekly fixed (rent share + core payroll + software)4,200,000
Tickets needed before ads300 / week
If ads add 400k and no extra ticketsBreak-even moves out

4,200,000 ÷ 14,000 = 300. Ads that do not buy incremental contribution are just a higher hurdle.

Arithmetic on the board if needed. Then studio.

Studio

Canvas + three assumptions

20 minutes · groups of 4

Take the Medan business you have been carrying, or a new idea. Fill nine blocks. Circle three assumptions (desire, feasibility, viability). For each: the test, the metric, the kill-criteria.

Kill-criteria example: ‘if fewer than 15 weekday cups at Rp 28k after two weeks, drop the office-commuter bet.’ Hear two groups.
